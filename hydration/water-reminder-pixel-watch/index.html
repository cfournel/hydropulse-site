A water reminder for Google Pixel Watch

Pixel Watch owners often ask how to log water and get reminders from the watch itself. HydroPulse does both on the wrist, and decides when to remind you from your heart rate and the heat rather than a fixed hour.

Why use it on a Pixel Watch

Water tracking in the Fitbit ecosystem has mostly lived in the phone app. HydroPulse keeps it on the watch: a tile with your balance, a notification with a Log water button, and a history of the day.

Its reminders are not hourly. They follow a running estimate of your losses, built from the watch's heart-rate sensor, your activity and the local weather, so they come early on a hot workout day and stay quiet on a calm one.

Drinks can be written to Health Connect, where other health apps can read them.

What HydroPulse does here

  • Pixel Watch, Pixel Watch 2, 3 and 4 (Wear OS 3 or newer).
  • A tile, a complication and notification actions.
  • Works without the phone nearby: the model and the log live on the watch.

How HydroPulse decides

  1. You enter your weight, age and sweat profile once. That sets your personal baseline loss — the water you lose just by being alive and breathing.
  2. The watch reads your heart rate continuously and converts it into a heart-rate-reserve fraction: how hard you are working relative to your own resting and maximum heart rate, not to an average person's.
  3. Local temperature and humidity are fetched anonymously and turned into a heat-index multiplier, because the same effort costs far more fluid at 33 °C and 70 % humidity than at 18 °C.
  4. Every drink you log is credited back to the ledger. When the running balance crosses your threshold, HydroPulse tells you — with a specific millilitre amount and the reason behind it.

What to expect

Install it from Google Play on the watch, set your weight and units once, and allow heart rate, activity and approximate location. Battery use is small: passive heart-rate data and a periodic check, with the weather cached.

Wear OS 3.0+. One-time purchase — no subscription, no ads, no account. The app is in English, French, Spanish, German and Portuguese.

Common questions

Can I log water on Pixel Watch?

Yes, with HydroPulse: from its tile, its notification or the app, in one tap.

Does it sync with Fitbit?

HydroPulse writes your drinks to Health Connect. Whether the Fitbit app shows them depends on Fitbit's own Health Connect support on your phone.

Does it need the phone?

No. Everything runs on the watch; only the weather lookup needs a connection.

Where the model comes from

The baseline draws on IOM and EFSA adequate-intake figures, the sweat-rate component on ACSM exercise-hydration guidance, and the environmental multiplier on the US National Weather Service heat-index formula. These are population references adapted to what a watch can actually sense — a well-founded estimate, not a laboratory measurement.

Not a medical device

HydroPulse produces an estimate, not a measurement. It is a wellness tool, not a medical device, and it does not diagnose, treat or monitor dehydration or any other condition. If you take diuretics, restrict fluid on medical advice, or have a kidney, heart or endocrine condition, follow your clinician's intake guidance rather than this app's. Seek urgent care for confusion, fainting, or heat illness.

Private by design

Your heart rate, activity and drink log never leave the watch. There is no account, no advertising SDK and no product analytics — nothing tracks what you do in the app. Two things do leave the device: a pair of coordinates to a public weather service, with no identifier attached, and a crash report if the app fails. Neither carries your health data.
